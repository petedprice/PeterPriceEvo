About

I'm a postdoctoral researcher in the Wright Lab at the University of Sheffield, working on the genetic conflicts that shape sexual traits. I did my PhD here too, on conflict and competition in the evolution of sexual traits, and before that an MSci in human genetics at UCL, working on computational methods for detecting positive selection. My work moves between population genomics, single-cell approaches, and long-read sequencing, in systems from stalk-eyed flies to zebra finches to chickens.

Research

Three threads run through my current work, all circling the same question: how does conflict between the sexes shape genomes.

Taeniopygia guttata

Sex-linked inversions

Tracing the molecular evolution of a sex-linked inversion polymorphism in zebra finches, and what it reveals about the maintenance of genetic variation on sex chromosomes.

Teleopsis dalmanni

Meiotic drive

Using single-cell approaches to characterise the consequences of X-linked meiotic drive in stalk-eyed flies, work grounded in fieldwork collecting samples in Peninsula Malaysia.

Comparative genomics

Selection on gene expression

Developing and applying methods to detect signatures of selection on gene expression, and mapping the genomic architecture of sexual conflict more broadly.
